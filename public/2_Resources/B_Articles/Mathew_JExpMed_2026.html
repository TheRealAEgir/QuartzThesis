Notes

Summary : This article studied CD4+ TRM response in the nasal tissue (NT) following influenza infection. Infection generated antigen-dependent stable population of TRM in NT and lung with low proliferation. Challenge expanded the cells, and depletion of CD4 lead to poorer survival to the challenge (although not significative). scRNAseq analyses revealed some differences between NT/Lung, and that antigen specificity could dirve Th1/Th17 vs cytotoxic/Tfh differenciation. Clonality was evenly distributed, with some biases relative to organ. NT TRM had higher expression of CXCR6. Co-transfer of CXCR6 WT/KO BM T cells lead to increase of WT in the tissues, and increase of KO in blood and SLO. There was co-localization of CXCL16 with CXCR6, and treatment of mouse with anti-CXCL16 reduced number of cells in tissues. Human NT TRM had CD103/CXCR6 expression, with low IFN/TNA but IL17 secretion. Conditional depletion of Th17 (via CD4creRORgtflox) or anti-IL17 treatment did show some increased virus titer, and lower tissue repair (with more apoptotic cells), suggesting a partial role for Th17 in IAV infection. Experiment : Transfer of marked OT-II T CD4+ T cells in mice and infection with influenza A virus expressing OVA (IAV-OVA) + intravascular injection of anti-CD4. Results : There was similar frequency of CD44+CD62L-CD69+ TRM in nasal tissue (NT) and lung, but lower frequency in nasal-associated lymphoid tissue (NALT). There was some expression of CD103 and higher in NT compared to lung. Nearly all TRM expressed CD11a. Some cell were antigen-specific from 10 dpi, and decreased until a stable population. NT TRM had low homeostatic proliferation. Antigen was necessary for TRM formation in NT and lung. Both NT/lung TRM produced IL2/IFN/TNF, but lung produced higer amount despite ICOS being more expressed on NT. PD1 expression was similar

Experiment : Infection of mouse with PR8, and challenge with X31, with has different surface but same internal protein, and FTY720 treatment. Results : There was enrichment of T cells specific of the second antigen after challenge, that was not due to expansion of naive T cells. Depletion of CD8 did not change the virus titer but depletion of CD4 increased it to the level of mouse without a primary infection. Infection to the lung made antigen-specific T cells only forming in the lung.

Experiment : Intra-nasal vaccination of mouse before IAV injection. Results : This formed TRM in NT and cLN that persited for at least 50 dpi, but almost no TRM in lung and mLN. This improved the survival of mouse after injection of lethal dose of virus, but did not change virus titer. There was a trend toward loss of the benefit when depleting CD4.

Experiment : scRNA TCRseq of sorted antigen-specific resident CD4+ T cells after 30 dpi. Results : Antigen-specific TRM had no difference in cell type proportion than total CD4, except for a loss of Th17 in the lung. However, NP-specific were mostly Th17 and Th1, while HA-specific were mainly cytotoxic and Tfh. Lung Th1 had higher CCR2, CTLA4, IFNg and NFkBIA but less CREM, TAF7 and XCL1 than NT Th1. NT had high expression of CXCR6 and CCR8 (CD4, Tfh, Th17), and NT Th1 had higher TFF1/XCL1 and lower CREM/NR4A2 compared to NT Th17. Other Results : TCR clonality was distrubuted evenly except for lung naive/Treg/NKT/Th17 which were mainly single clones. NT Th17 was more expanded. Clonotype bias was only prevent relative to organ-specific, meaning that some clonotype could be locally expanded.

Other Results : By looking specifically at CXCR6 expression; it was higher in NT than lung and in Tfh/Th17/Treg/early activated. Infected and naive mouse had the same expression of CXCR6 in NT; this was surprising since the naive mouse should had TRM, but was interpreted as mouse having previous insult.

Experiment : Co-transfer of BM WT of CXCR6 KO. Results : There was higher CXCR6 KO leukocytes in the blood. Following infection, there was more antigen-specific CD4 TRM in NT and lung from CXCR6 WT at 30 dpi and more CXCR6 KO in blood/spleen/tdmLN. Other Results : There was colocalization of CXCR6 with CXCL16 in NT. Treatment of mouse with anti-CXCL16 lead to reduced number of NT and some reduction in lung.

Experiment : Analyses from human NT/PBMC Results : There was TRM in NT which expressed some CD103 and CXCR6. PBMC secreted IFN but not TRM, with no TNF secretion after stimulation with IAV protein, but some IL17 secretion (even without stimulation)

Experiment : Conditionnal depletion of RORGT in CD4 T cells and evaluation of the importance of Th17 in the challenge to IAV in mouse (with FTY720 treatment before the challenge). Results : There was slight increase in virus titer, and increased tissue damage (apoptoic cells) in NT. There was no effect of depletion of IL17.

Mathew, N. R., Gailleton, R., Scharf, L., Schön, K., Enriquez, J., Axelsson, H., Strömberg, A., Lycke, N., Bemark, M., Tang, K.-W., & Angeletti, D. (2026). Nasal CD4+ tissue-resident memory T cells provide cross-protective immunity to influenza.

Abstract

CD4 tissue-resident memory T cells (TRM) are crucial adaptive immune components involved in preventing influenza A virus (IAV) infection. Despite their importance, their physiological role in the upper respiratory tract, the first site of contact with IAV, remains unclear. Here, we find that, after IAV infection, antigen-specific CD4 TRM persist in the nasal tissue (NT) compartment after infection and provide protection upon heterosubtypic challenge. Single-cell RNA-sequencing analysis reveals that NT CD4 TRM are heterogeneous and transcriptionally distinct as compared with their lung counterparts. Mechanistically, we demonstrate that the CXCR6–CXCL16 axis promotes CD4 TRM residency in the NT. Furthermore, we show that the NT of mice and humans contains a high frequency of Th17 CD4 TRM that aid in local viral clearance and in reducing tissue damage. Collectively, our results support a robust physiological role for NT CD4 TRM in local protection during heterosubtypic IAV infection. .

Highlight

The major T cell subpopulation was, as expected given the intracellular viral infection, Th1 cells (Fig. S4 B) defined by a well-established signature (Andreatta et al., 2022), including Ifng and Cxcr3. Cytotoxic CD4 T cells were present in both organs and defined by Nkg7, Fasl, Ccl4, Ccl5, and a CD4 cytotoxic signature (Hashimoto et al., 2019). T follicular helper (Tfh) cells were generally more prevalent within lungs (Fig. S4 B), consistent with a possible role in supporting ectopic germinal centers in iBALTs. Based on their transcriptional profile, they were separated into two clusters, named Tfh.1 and Tfh.2. Tfh.1 were Tfh cells with strong expression of Tnfsf8, Bcl6, and a signature previously associated with Tfh (Andreatta et al., 2022). Nevertheless, Tfh.2 had a higher expression of other Tfhassociated genes such as Tox, Slamf6, and Pdcd1. Quiescent memory cells were slightly more prevalent within NT and characterized by the expression of several long noncoding RNAs (including Malat1) (Dey et al., 2023; Plasek and Valadkhan, 2021) and low ribosomal gene expression. A small proportion of our CD4 TRM were na ̈ıve-like T cells expressing Ccr7, Sell, and Klf2 (Wang et al., 2022). Furthermore, we identified regulatory T cells (Tregs) by Foxp3 expression and Treg signature (Yang et al., 2021). Early activated cells were defined based on the expression of genes linked with migration within the tissue, such as Gpr183 (Li et al., 2016), and signaling receptors such as Ms4a4b (Yan et al., 2012), suggesting an overall movement within the tissue but also modulation of T cell activation. An “acutely activated” cluster expressed Cd40lg, several transcription factors and also had high expression of genes involved in regulation of NF-κB signaling (Nfkbia, Nfkbid, and Nfkbiz).
