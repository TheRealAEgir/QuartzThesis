Notes

Summary : This article showed that Treg-secreted IL10 had an important role in the formation of a memory T cell during the inflammation resolution phase. IL10 KO mouse showed impairement of memory T cells (characterized as CD127+KLRG1-, with expression of CXCR3, CD27, CD62L) and more KLRG1+CD127- TE cells. This was also found when treatment with IL10 neutralizing antibody injection when treated at the infection initiation or at memory timepoint 15, but not at 8 dpi. No effect was observed when using IL10RA KO T cells. IL10 was secreted by Treg, because use of IL10 DC/myeloid had no effect, and it was shown that Treg had IL10 secretion and effect were recapitulated when using IL10 KO under the promoter of FOXP3. Transcriptome analysis validated the result of Treg-secreted IL10 absence on the transcriptome of memory T cells. There also was effect on DC maturation (higher maturation) and elevated serum DC secreted cytokine concentration. Effect were recapitulated when depleting all Treg, also at infection start and after 15 dpi. Finally, effect where partially restaured when transfering IL10-sufficient Treg in IL10 Ko mouse. Effector_Memory cell formation.

Experiment : Infection of WT/IL10 KO mouse with LCMV Results : There was differences in hte composition of the obtained splenic memory T cell pool; there was more KLRG1+CD127 in the KO mouse and less CD127+KLRG1-CXCR3+CD27+CD62L+ with TCF1 MP. Experiment : Neutralizing IL10 antibody injection Results : Similar results when treating during all phase of infection. Results disappeared when doing treatment before 8 dpi or after 15 dpi, suggesting an effect at the initiation of the memory phase.

Experiment : Use of IL10RA conditionnal T cell KO, transfer in a mouse then infection with LCMV. Results : There was no impairement in the formation of KLRG1-CD127+ and CD62L+KLRG1- MP, but there was T cell at memory timepoint.

Experiment : WT or IL10 KO mouse infected with LCMV and administration of TLR9 agonist CpG B at 8 or 15 dpi Results : KO mouse had greater loss of KLRG1-CD127+ and KLRG1+CD127- when treated at 8 but not 15 dpi.

Experiment : Mouse with IL10 KO specificaly in DC (CD11c), myeloid (Lyz2) of T cells (CD4) Results : Deletion of DC or myeloid had little impact on the maturation of memory T cells, but deletion of T cells mediated IL10 resulted in loss of KLRG1-CD127+ and CD62L+KLRG1- memory T cells.

Experiment : Use of IL10 reporter 10BiT mouse strain that express Thy1.1 upon IL10 expression; Results : Only Treg kept IL10 expresion at iflammation resolution phase. These Treg were also more activated and were protected for intravascular CD4 staining (meaning spleen white pulp).

Experiment : IL10 specific KO in Treg (FOXP3) Results : There was no difference in cell number, but KO mouse had more KLRG1+CD127- and less CD62L+KLRG1- and lower expression of CXCR3, CD27 and TCF1.

Experiment : Generation of LCMV immune WT or IL10 Treg KO mouse and re-challenge with gp33-LM to test memory response. Results : There was no reduction in bacterial burden, but reduction in proliferation of the Treg in the KO condition.

Experiment : Transcriptome analysis of the memory T cells in the IL10 WT/KO Treg conditions. Results : Gene associated in formation/function of memory T cells, pro/anti inflammatory genes, GSEA memory and pro-inflammatory gene signatures and exposition to IL12 and type I interferons and TLR stimulation.

Other Results : Study of DC maturation in the IL10 Treg KO mouse showed that DC where more mature with CD89, CD86, PD-L1, PD-L2 expression and higher concentration of DC-secreted cytokines such as IL6, IL1B, TNF, IL12p70 in the mouse serum.

Experiment : Use of FOXP3 GFP-DTR for depletion of Treg upon diphteria toxin treatment mouse. Results : There was an effect on depletion of Treg when treated at the time of infection or at 8 dpi, but not after 15 dpi on the memory T cell formation.

Experiment : Transfer of IL10-sufficient Treg in IL10 KO mouse Results : There was a restoration of the maturation of DC and the formation of CD127+ TCF1+ KLRG1- GZMB- memory T cells compared to mouse with no Treg transfer.

Abstract

The precise factors that control effective memory formation by T cells are unclear. Kaech et al. demonstrate that regulatory T cell–produced IL-10 is critical for the generation of CD8+ memory cells. .
