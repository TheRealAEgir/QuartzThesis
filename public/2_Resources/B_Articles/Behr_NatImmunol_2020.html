Summary : This article used HOBIT fate mapping and described TRM Recirculation in MLN from intestine infection. Secondary infection of primary Trm gave rise to an exHOBIT population that mostly resemble Tem, but are distinct from regular Tem. Transfer of Trm in mice only lead to Trm and Tem formation as opposed to Tcm which gave rise to every populations. Removal of primary Trm before secondary response impaired Trm and Tem formation, suggesting that primary Trm plays a role in secondary Trm and Tem formation. Model : Use of a fate mapping model with tdTomato, cre recombinase and diphteria toxin receptor in the HOBIT locus on OT-I cells in an oral infection with listeria monocytogenes-OVA Results : Hobit reporter T cells differenciated in Tcm/Tem in the spleen, MLN and liver, as well as CD69+ Trm in the liver and intestine LP and IEL. Only Trm had expression of HOBIT and reporter gene.

Experiment : Secondary infection with LM-OVA Results : Rapid formation of Trm expressing HOBIT in the intestine. CD69-HOBIT+ cells were detected in MLN at 8 dpi

Model : ROSA26-flox-stop-flox-eYFP and CRE under HOBIT promoter, which make cell that had expressed HOBIT once permanently express YFP Results : ex vivo stimulation with OVA lead to downregulation of HOBIT. Reinfection of mice lead to the apparition of an exHOBIT CD69-CD62L-+ (~Tcm) or KLRG1+CX3CR1+ (~Tem) population in the blood which could be derived from HOBIT+ Trm. There also was the apparition of an HOBIT+ Trm population in MLN, which was CD69+CD62L-.

Experiment : Transcriptional analyses of exHOBIT Results : exHOBIT clustered with Tem populations with downregulation of Trm genes (XCL1, RGS1, OSGIN1, P2RX7) and upregulation of S1PR1, S1PR5, KLF2. Compared with Tem, exHOBIT downregulated EOMES and DAPL1, which are downregulated by memory T cells after antigen stimulation.

Experiment : Co transfer of exHOBIT and YFP- Tem in mice and LM-OVA infection Results : No difference in bacterial burden in spleen, but exHOBIT showed higher control in intestine and MLN.

Experiment : Co transfer of Tcm and liver Trm into mice and re-infection LCMV Results : Tcm lead to effector Tcm in almost all tissue site, and Trm almost only on the liver. After another challenge, both Tcm and Trm formed responses in blood, spleen and liver with high proliferation of effector cells. Trm were unable to form Tcm and were absent from lymph node, and trm derived memory T cells in the spleen were KLRG1+CX3CR1+. Similar results with Trm cells from intestine.

Model : Removing of HOBIT expressing OT-I with diphteria toxin injection (DTR expressin through HOBIT promoter) Results : HOBIT+ cells were removed from liver and intestine, while Tcm was unaffected. This impaired KLRG1+ Tem compartiment as well.

Model : OT-I P2RX7 KO or WT co-transfer, which does not impair Trm/Tcm and Tem in LM-OVA infection. NAD treatment lead to specific depletion of Trm and retain Tcm and Tem only on P2RX7 WT. Results : Removal of primary Trm lead to impaired formation of Trm but also KLRG1+ Tem upon secondary infection.

Abstract

Tissue-resident memory CD8+ T cells (TRM cells) are crucial in protecting against reinvading pathogens, but the impact of reinfection on their tissue confinement and contribution to recall responses is unclear. We developed a unique lineage tracer mouse model exploiting the TRM-defining transcription factor homolog of Blimp-1 in T cells (Hobit) to fate map the TRM progeny in secondary responses. After reinfection, a sizeable fraction of secondary memory T cells in the circulation developed downstream of TRM cells. These tissue-experienced ex-TRM cells shared phenotypic properties with the effector memory T cell population but were transcriptionally and functionally distinct from other secondary effector memory T cell cells. Adoptive transfer experiments of TRM cells corroborated their potential to form circulating effector and memory cells during recall responses. Moreover, specific ablation of primary TRM cell populations substantially impaired the secondary T cell response, both locally and systemically. Thus, TRM cells retain developmental plasticity and shape both local and systemic T cell responses on reinfection. .
