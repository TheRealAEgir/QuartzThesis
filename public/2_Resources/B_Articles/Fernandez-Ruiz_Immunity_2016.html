Notes

Summary : This article studied TRM formation in the liver in a malaria vaccination setting, and showed that TRM confer protection to malaria. They observed CD69+/KLRG1-/CD103- resident-verified TRM and CD69-/KLRG1+ TEM in the liver following the vaccine. Two-photon microscopy showed that TRM were ameoboid and travelled slowly in the sinusoid, whereas TEM were round and traveled faster. The use of a vaccination strategy consisting of priming by DC (peptide-Ab anti CLEC9A + CpG) and recruitement to the liver (infection with adenovirus expressing peptide only in hepatocyte) showed higher resident-verified TRM infiltration and protection. Trm had higher positive correlation between cell number and disease control than Tem and higher functionnality/cytotoxicity. Depletion of TRM with anti-CXCR3 lead to the loss of the protection. Experiment : GFP+ PbT-I T cells specific for malaria pathogenes in mice vaccinated with radiation-attenuated sporozoites (RAS) and analyses of spleen/liver T cells 31 dpi. Results : There was KLRG1+-/CXCR6+- populations in the liver, but only KLRG1+CXCR6- in the spleen. The liver population expressed CD69 and was long lived. Transcriptomic showed that liver CD69-KLRG1+ ressembled antigen-specific spleen T cells, suggesting that they were TEM. CD69+KLRG1- ressembled TRM cells but lacked CD103 expression.

Experiment : Transfer of liver T cells in mice. TRM died unless using nanobody s+16a which block ARTC2.2, which sense NAD and ADP-ribosylate P2RX7 causing cell death. Results : Transfered cells preferentially homed to the liver. Parabiosis experiment showed that these cells were resident.

Experiment : Intra vascular staining and two-photon microscopy Results : Liver T cells were labelled, suggesting that they were blood exposed. Microscopy revealed that they were located at liver sinusoid with slow migration.

Experiment : prime-and-trap vaccination stategy; peptide coupled to mAb anti-CLEC9A to target DC with poly(I:C) or CpG; then T cells are attracted to the liver by using recombinant adeno-associated virus that express the T cell antigen upon hepatocyte-specific alpha-1 antitrypsin promoter. Results : There was formation of liver TRM cells that protected upon challenge. CpG was more efficient than poly(I:C). Parabiosis confirmed residency. Trm had higher positive correlation between cell number and disease control than Tem.

Experiment : Use of GFP-CX3CR1 and GFP-CXCR6 to track Tem and Trm, respectively. Results : Microscopy showed that Tem were rounder and travelled faster in the liver. Trm were amoeboid and migrated slowly in the sinusoids.

Experiment : Use of anti-CXCR3 to specifically deplete TRM but not TEM (verified) Results : Treatement lead to the loss of protection; this was not lost in NKT-less deficient mice (because NKT also epxress CXCR3)

Experiment : ex-vivo re-stimulation to study functionnality/cytotoxicity. Had to use nanobody s+16a here also. Results : TRM had better functionnality/cytotoxicity than TEM.

Experiment : Use of a mathematical model to predict how much time and how many TRM are necessary for liver control. 2.5 Trm necessary for 99% coverage in 48 hours (mouse).

Useful

Tse et al (2014) observed high expression of CXCR6 in liver-associated CD8+ T cells after vaccination using radiation-attenuated sporozoites against malaria.

Highlight

Analyses of liver and splenic PbT-I cells 34–36 days after vaccination, once viral antigen was cleared from the liver (Figure S4), revealed effects of both adjuvant and viral components on memory T cell formation (Figures 4B, 4D, and 4E). In the absence of the rAAV component, Clec9A-targeted priming with CpG was superior to poly(I:C) in forming liver Trm cells (Figures 4B and 4D, left graph). When such priming was combined with rAAV-NVY (and therefore hepatic antigen expression), a greater number of Trm cells were induced, with CpG again superior to poly(I:C)

One marker, CXCR3, was expressed by all liver Trm cells and was largely absent from Tem cells (Figure 6B). Injection of mAb to this marker into prime-and-trap vaccinated mice on days 32 and 34 after vaccination revealed almost complete depletion of liver Trm cells by flow cytometry (Figures 6C–6F) and a loss of cells of a patrolling phenotype by intravital imaging (Movie S7) with no significant loss of splenic or liver Tem cells

Highlight

Consistent with this conclusion, protection was also lost after depleting mice of CD8+ cells (Figure 6H) but was not lost in CD1d-deficient mice, which lack NKT cells, another CXCR3+ liver cell type (Figure 6I), nor in mice depleted of a combination of CD4+ and NK1.1+ cells, which would include NKT and NK cells

Highlight

This model estimated that approximately 2.5 million Trm cells were required for 99% coverage within the critical 48 hr window of liver-stage infection. Real values (Figure 6A) suggest that approximately one-tenth this number of Trm cells (as detected by flow cytometry) were 100% protective. Because flow cytometry has been reported to detect only about 20% of T cells in the liver (Steinert et al., 2015), these real numbers approach those predicted in the model for surveillance of 99% of the liver.
